You may be posting regularly without knowing whether you are reaching people who could use your product.

## Look at who is there

Being able to post in a community does not mean its members are likely to buy from you. Look at what they do, what they need and whether your product fits.

Other founders may be customers too. It depends on what you sell.

## Ask these questions

<div class="table-scroll" role="region" aria-label="Questions to ask about a community" tabindex="0">

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Do members have the problem you solve? | Their interest in the topic may not mean they need your product |
| Can they decide to buy it? | The person using a product may not control the budget |
| What do they use now? | Their current choice gives you something to compare with |
| Can you take part under the community's rules? | You need a suitable way to talk with people |
| What happens after someone shows interest? | A conversation, trial or purchase tells you more than a like |

</div>

## Consider other reasons for a poor response

You may be reaching the wrong people. Your explanation may be unclear. People may not trust the offer or feel a need to act now. The product itself may not be useful enough.

The amount of time you spend posting does not tell you which reason applies.

## Try a small test

Choose one type of customer and one problem they need to solve. Find a community where people discuss that problem. Follow its rules and take part in a useful way.

Decide what you want to learn. Record relevant conversations, product trials or purchases, depending on the test. Likes alone will not answer whether people need the product.

If little happens, check whether you reached the intended people before ruling out the whole community.

## What remains uncertain

Finding possible customers does not mean you can reach enough of them at a reasonable cost. People who need the product may not have the budget to buy it.

One founder's success with replies does not mean the same approach will work for you.
